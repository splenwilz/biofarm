At Badger Bank Farm, we’re transforming 30 hectares of predominantly arable land into a thriving mix of grassland, scrub, trees and hedgerow. Set beside ancient woodland and close to the River Ure corridor, Badger Bank will help nature thrive beyond its boundaries. Lesnewth Skiddy Activation - APPROVED

From the ground: the real work starts at Lesnewth
At Lesnewth, our ecology and land teams met with the landowner, grazier and Cornwall Wildflower CIC to plan the habitat bank’s first activation: establishing 3ha of wildflower meadow.
[Read the full details]
Standing in the field, the team worked through the practical details: how to prepare the ground, when to sow and, importantly, what should go into the seed mix.
Rather than using a standard wildflower mix, the seed is being carefully selected to suit the local conditions and character of this part of North Cornwall. It’s about creating the right meadow, in the right place.
Over the coming weeks, the field will be prepared and sown, before the cattle stay off through the winter to give the meadow time to establish.
This is the first activation at Lesnewth and the beginning of the habitat restoration planned across the wider habitat bank. Over time, wildflower grassland will sit alongside enhanced woodland, scrub and Cornish hedgerows, creating stronger connections for nature across the landscape.
For us, this is what long term BNG delivery looks like on the ground: bringing together ecological expertise and the people who know the land to make the right decisions for the landscape.

BNG fact file: what changed and what stayed
BNG rules changed over the summer. The system is now more proportionate, but the 10% requirement and market for offsite units remain firmly in place.
The 10% requirement still stands
	•	Qualifying developments in England must deliver at least 10% BNG.
	•	The main exemptions include home extensions and loft conversions, work permitted without a full planning application, sites of 0.2 hectares or less and temporary developments of five years or less.
	•	Priority habitat protections still apply.
	•	Self-build and custom-build developments are no longer automatically exempt.
Offsite BNG is more accessible
	•	Onsite habitat creation, onsite enhancement and registered offsite gains now have equal priority.
	•	This applies to non-major developments (fewer than 10 homes or smaller commercial schemes below the major development thresholds).
	•	Developers can choose the most practical route from the outset.
	•	This supports continued demand for high integrity units and long-term opportunities for landowners.
	•	Statutory credits remain the last resort.
Major infrastructure is next
	•	From 2 November 2026, BNG will become mandatory for Nationally Significant Infrastructure Project applications.
	•	This includes major energy, transport and water schemes.
More flexibility for development. More opportunity for nature.

A smarter BNG solution supporting major investment
A bespoke BNG solution helped keep a major programme of renovation and construction moving at a significant aviation technology site in Rochester, while delivering the right outcome for nature.
[Read the full article]
The site is undergoing a £220 million transformation, including extensive renovation and the construction of a new 32,000 square metre advanced technology factory.
It is home to a 1,600 strong workforce and develops advanced technologies for civil and military aircraft. It also provides year-round repairs for commercial airliners and is an important source of skilled employment in Medway. The investment is expected to create a further 300 jobs.
To support the wider programme, the project team needed to bring additional land into operational use. But the land included a nationally important open mosaic habitat, creating a complex biodiversity challenge.
Working alongside town planning and environmental consultancy Cassidy & Ashton, Biofarm designed an integrated solution that enabled the client to deliver its BNG requirement both onsite and offsite.
Planned habitat delivery on the development site would meet part of the requirement. Biofarm brought this together with the right mix of offsite biodiversity units to meet the remaining need, securing long term habitat creation and management through a registered habitat bank.
By looking at the project as a whole, the solution significantly reduced the number of offsite units the client needed to purchase and, in turn, the overall cost of delivering BNG.
The result worked for the client and for nature. It provided a clear route through the biodiversity requirements, helped the wider programme progress and directed long term investment into habitat creation where it could deliver the greatest benefit.
Alban Cassidy, Director at Cassidy & Ashton, said:
“This was a complex project with important operational, commercial and ecological considerations. Biofarm took the time to understand the project as a whole and designed a solution that brought the onsite and offsite elements together. It reduced the client’s offsite BNG requirement and costs, while providing a practical route forward for a major programme of investment.”

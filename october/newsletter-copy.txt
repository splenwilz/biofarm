Slades Farm – Awaiting approval

Local by nature | Slades Farm, Surrey
Across 88 hectares near Bramley, south of Guildford, Slades Farm is creating more space for nature in the heart of Surrey’s Wealden Greensand.
It’s a landscape of open pasture, wooded slopes, copses and established hedgerows. Our plans build on that character, creating and enhancing wildflower rich grassland, mixed scrub, individual trees and lowland mixed deciduous woodland.
By retaining existing hedgerows and connecting habitats across the farm, we’ll create more food, shelter and nesting opportunities for birds, bats and invertebrates, while giving wildlife better routes through the wider countryside.
Local LPA: Waverley Borough Council
NCA: Wealden Greensand
BNG registration: BGS-060325001

A long term future for the farm
For landowner Ed Hutley, Slades is about giving the land a future that works for the farm and for nature.
“We wanted to find a long-term future for the land that made sense for the farm and for nature. Working with Biofarm gives us the ecological expertise and support to make that happen, creating more space for wildlife while building on what makes this landscape special.”
Ed Hutley, Landowner, Slades Farm
Over the next 30 years, we’ll work alongside Ed to manage the habitats as they establish and evolve, bringing together his knowledge of the farm with our ecological expertise.

BNG rooted in the local landscape
Slades Farm brings 350 BNG units to Surrey across individual rural trees, lowland mixed deciduous woodland, mixed scrub and other neutral grassland.
For developments in the area, that means locally available BNG backed by long term management, while the investment supports the creation and restoration of habitat here at Slades.

Slades at Sleight
Learning from the land, together
We recently welcomed Ed Hutley, owner of Slades Farm in Surrey and one of our newest registered habitat bank partners, to Sleight Farm in Bath and North East Somerset.
Ed was keen to see how cattle and ponies are helping restore habitats through conservation grazing. We explored how their natural grazing behaviours, combined with rotational grazing across the farm’s 72 hectares, can reduce nutrient levels, create a more varied grassland structure and support the return of species-rich wildflower meadows.
Visits like this are an important part of how we work with our landowner partners. They bring people together to share first-hand experience, exchange ideas and see habitat restoration in action, drawing on the knowledge and expertise across our growing community of habitat banks.

NSIPs
The BNG market is growing
From 2 November 2026, Biodiversity Net Gain (BNG) becomes mandatory for [Nationally Significant Infrastructure Projects] (NSIPs) in England.
It’s another important step in the evolution of BNG. Some of the country’s biggest infrastructure projects will join housing and commercial developments already delivering mandatory BNG, broadening the range and scale of development contributing to habitat creation and restoration.
For [landowners], it could bring new opportunities for the right land to play a part. With major infrastructure projects able to consider on-site and off-site habitat delivery from the outset, habitat banks could have an important role to play. These projects can have different and potentially larger BNG requirements, creating opportunities to think about habitat at a landscape scale and strengthen connections with nature around it.
For [developers] already navigating BNG, it’s another sign of a market continuing to mature. As demand broadens, having the right habitats available in the right places will matter. Thinking about BNG early gives teams more options and more time to find the right fit for their development.
And there’s a bigger picture here too. Bringing major infrastructure into BNG has the potential to create and restore more habitat, connect landscapes and extend nature recovery across some of the places experiencing the greatest change.
Our growing network of [habitat banks] is already creating and restoring habitats across England, connecting landowners with developments that need locally relevant BNG.
If you’d like to understand more about what this next phase of BNG could mean in practice, our team is always happy to [talk].

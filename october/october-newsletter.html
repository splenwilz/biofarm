Local by nature | Slades Farm, Surrey

Across 88 hectares near Bramley, south of Guildford, Slades Farm is creating more space for nature in the heart of Surrey’s Wealden Greensand.

It’s a landscape of open pasture, wooded slopes, copses and established hedgerows. Our plans build on that character, creating and enhancing wildflower rich grassland, mixed scrub, individual trees and lowland mixed deciduous woodland.

By retaining existing hedgerows and connecting habitats across the farm, we’ll create more food, shelter and nesting opportunities for birds, bats and invertebrates, while giving wildlife better routes through the wider countryside.

Local LPA: Waverley Borough Council
NCA: Wealden Greensand
BNG registration: BGS-060325001

A long term future for the farm

For landowner Ed Hutley, the decision to partner with Biofarm was driven by a desire to secure the farm’s future while creating more space for nature.

“We wanted to find a long-term future for the land that made sense for the farm and for nature. Working with Biofarm gives us the ecological expertise and support to make that happen, creating more space for wildlife while building on what makes this landscape special.”

Over the next 30 years, we’ll work alongside Ed to manage the habitats as they establish and evolve, bringing together his knowledge of the farm with our ecological expertise.

BNG rooted in the local landscape

Slades Farm brings 350 BNG units to Surrey across individual rural trees, lowland mixed deciduous woodland, mixed scrub and other neutral grassland.

For developments in the area, that means locally available BNG backed by long term management, while the investment supports the creation and restoration of habitat here at Slades.

A new chapter for infrastructure BNG

From 2 November 2026, BNG becomes mandatory for Nationally Significant Infrastructure Projects (NSIPs) in England.

It marks an important next step for BNG, extending its reach to some of the country’s largest infrastructure projects and creating new opportunities for habitat creation and restoration.

Celebrating our Rising Star finalist

We’re delighted that our Senior Ecologist, Evie Hymas, was named a finalist for Rising Star of the Year – SME at the 2026 Women in Green Business Awards.

The awards celebrate women driving change across the green economy and attracted more than 1,000 nominations this year. Evie was selected as one of just nine finalists in her category, making her recognition a huge achievement.

Evie works across our habitat banks, helping turn ambitious habitat plans into action on the ground. Learning from the land, together

We recently welcomed Ed Hutley, owner of Slades Farm in Surrey and one of our newest registered habitat bank partners, to Sleight Farm in Bath and North East Somerset.

Ed was keen to see how cattle and ponies are helping restore habitats through conservation grazing. We explored how their natural grazing behaviours, combined with rotational grazing across the farm’s 70 hectares, can reduce nutrient levels, create a more varied grassland structure and support the return of species-rich wildflower meadows.

Visits like this are an important part of how we work with our landowner partners. They bring people together to share first-hand experience, exchange ideas and see habitat restoration in action, drawing on the knowledge and expertise across our growing community of habitat banks.

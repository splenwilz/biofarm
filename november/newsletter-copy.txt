In Conversation | Dan Massie, The Hill Group

For housebuilders, BNG is now part of the everyday reality of development. But what does delivering it actually look like, beyond the regulations and the metric?

We caught up with Dan Massie, Development Director at The Hill Group, one of the UK's leading privately owned housebuilders, to hear how BNG is playing out from the developer's side of the table. We talked about its place in Hill's wider business strategy, the decisions behind on-site and off-site delivery, lessons learnt along the way and what makes a good BNG partner.

Local by nature | Avon Meadows, Warwickshire

Alongside the River Avon in Warwickshire, we’re restoring 17.4 hectares of riverside habitat to create a richer, more connected landscape for nature. With 110 registered units, Avon Meadows offers a local off-site BNG solution for developers across the region.

The River Avon is an important wildlife corridor, connecting habitats across Warwickshire and the wider catchment. Restoring land alongside it helps create more space for wildlife and strengthens the natural connections that riverside habitats depend on.

At Avon Meadows, we’re creating and enhancing grassland, mixed scrub, wet woodland, trees and river habitats. This work supports the priorities of Warwickshire’s Local Nature Recovery Strategy (LNRS), particularly the restoration of rivers, floodplains and connected habitats.

“The River Avon has always been an important part of this landscape. Working with Biofarm lets us restore the riverside habitats on our land and look after them properly for the long term.”

Nic Allen, Landowner, Avon Meadows

For developers, Avon Meadows offers registered BNG units backed by long-term ecological management and monitoring. Funding is ring-fenced for the full 30-year commitment, helping ensure habitats are established, maintained and monitored, and giving developers confidence in the long-term delivery of their BNG requirements.

Location: Stratford-on-Avon, Warwickshire Habitat bank area: 17.4 hectares Registered BNG units: 110

BNG moves up the agenda at Farm Business Innovation Show  [BUILT AS: "BNG moving up the agenda | Farm Business Innovation Show" - Chris, 19 Nov]

Tight farming margins and changes to grant funding were front of mind for many of the farmers and landowners we met at this year’s Farm Business Innovation Show.

We’ve attended the show for several years, and the conversations around biodiversity net gain (BNG) are becoming more practical. Landowners want to understand the potential financial returns, how agreements work and how nature restoration can fit alongside their existing farming operations.

It was encouraging to see growing interest in BNG as a practical way to generate long-term income while supporting the health and productivity of the land.

For developers, greater landowner engagement is good news too. It opens up more opportunities to create habitat banks in the locations where biodiversity units are needed, with the right habitats to meet local demand. That’s good for development, farming businesses and nature.

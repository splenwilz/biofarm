Overview

A landscape in recovery

Sleight Farm is a 70-hectare registered habitat bank in South West England. Once a heavily grazed sheep and goat farm, the site is being carefully managed to allow species-rich grassland to re-establish — creating high-quality, locally available Biodiversity Net Gain for nearby development.

Through conservation grazing, carefully timed hay cuts and ongoing monitoring, the grasslands are beginning to reveal what remains within the site’s natural seed bank. Recent surveys recorded lowland meadow indicator species and the first bee orchid on the site.

Location & areas served

Where it is, and where it can help

Sleight Farm is well placed to serve nearby local planning authorities across the South West. The solid area is the local zone — the host LPA (Bath & NE Somerset) and National Character Area (Bristol, Avon Valleys & Ridges) — where units apply at full value under the biodiversity gain spatial risk multiplier. The hatched area shows neighbouring LPAs and NCAs, where units remain eligible at an adjusted rate (0.75×). Beyond that, we deliver nationally.

Overview

A landscape in recovery

Once a heavily grazed sheep and goat farm, Sleight Farm is being carefully managed to allow species-rich grassland to re-establish — creating high-quality, locally available Biodiversity Net Gain for nearby development. Through conservation grazing, timed hay cuts and ongoing monitoring, recent surveys recorded lowland meadow indicator species and the first bee orchid on the site.

About the land & its legacy

From grazing land to living meadow

Rather than force change, our management creates the conditions for nature to recover — letting the grasslands re-establish and reveal the diversity held within the soil. Areas once classified as modified grassland are developing the characteristics of more diverse neutral grassland, while one parcel is moving towards lowland meadow.

The recovery so far

A landscape finding its character

At acquisition

A heavily grazed farm

Sleight Farm came to us as a sheep and goat farm, with relatively species-poor grassland and limited botanical diversity.

Management

Conservation grazing & hay cuts

We introduced conservation grazing and carefully timed hay cuts, creating the conditions for nature to recover.

Latest botanical survey

Meadow indicators & a first bee orchid

Knapweed, bird’s-foot trefoil, betony and pignut recorded — and the first bee orchid on the site. Read the survey →

Wildlife monitoring

Owls, badgers & bullfinches

Cameras recorded barn owl, a juvenile tawny owl, badgers, fallow deer and Amber-list bullfinches. See what we found →

Coming this autumn

Green-hay spreading

Green hay from the richest areas will be spread across the site to accelerate the spread of wildflowers.

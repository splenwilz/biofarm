# Habitat bank catchment areas as postcode districts (16 Oct 2026)

**What this is.** The companion to the full-value sheet (`service-area-postcode-districts.md`). For each of Biofarm's six registered habitat banks, the postcode districts (outward codes, e.g. BS22) that fall inside the bank's neighbouring area under the statutory biodiversity metric: the local planning authorities that border the bank's own LPA, plus the National Character Areas that border the bank's own NCA. Developments there can use the bank's units at the 0.75 spatial multiplier (Statutory Biodiversity Metric User Guide, July 2025, Table 9). This is the hatched area on each live bank page. Postcodes already inside the bank's own LPA or NCA are excluded, so the two sheets count every postcode once: a district can appear in both only where the LPA/NCA boundary cuts through it, and those districts are called out per bank below.

**How it was built.**

1. The neighbouring councils and character areas for each bank were taken from the live bank page's own map data (the `<BANK>_SRM` object embedded in each page's map code block, re-fetched 15 Oct 2026) and checked by adjacency against the ONS Local Authority Districts (May 2026, full-resolution) and Natural England National Character Areas layers: every council and character area listed touches the bank's own LPA or NCA, and no bordering council or character area is missing (`srm-adjacency-check.json`). The host LPA and NCA are those of the full-value sheet, derived from the grid reference on the public Biodiversity Gain Sites register (environment.data.gov.uk/biodiversity-net-gain, search API, re-fetched 15 Oct 2026, no changes).
2. Postcodes: every live postcode (no termination date) in each neighbouring council was pulled from the ONS Postcode Directory (August 2026); every live postcode centroid inside each neighbouring character area polygon was pulled from the ONSPD centroid layer by spatial query. The two sets were merged, overlap counted once, and every postcode already in the bank's full-value set (own LPA plus own NCA, as in the first sheet) was removed.
3. Districts were grouped and each district's share of its live postcodes inside the neighbouring area was computed against the district's national live-postcode total from the same directory, with the same core / partial / fringe thresholds as the first sheet.
4. Independent check against the live site: every postcode centroid in each list was tested against the hatched (neighbouring) and solid (own area) polygons drawn on the live bank page. The hit rates are given per bank. Misses are centroids that sit just outside the simplified web polygon, not postcodes outside the neighbouring councils or character areas.
5. National parks: the national park authorities are planning authorities in their own right, and the first sheet removed park postcodes from each bank's own council for that reason. The live maps do not make that distinction for neighbouring councils, and nor does this sheet: park postcodes inside a neighbouring council are kept and the counts are stated in the bank's flag so they can be taken out if preferred.
6. Non-geographic districts were identified the same way as in the first sheet: every live postcode in the district is coded large user (USRTYPIND = 1) and all of them sit on at most seven grid points, confirmed by pulling the whole district from the directory and against Royal Mail's published list of non-geographic codes (poweredbypaf.com, "Current non-geos", July 2019).

**Non-geographic districts (verified 16 Oct).** Thirty-seven districts in the lists are non-geographic and are shown separately under each bank as "do not target". Thirty-five meet the same test as in the first sheet: every live postcode in the district is coded large user in the ONS Postcode Directory (August 2026), zero small-user postcodes, and all of them sit on at most seven grid points, which is how the ONSPD user guide (section 11) records non-geographic postcodes (a grid reference at the sorting office, not a street); each whole district was pulled from the directory to check this, not just the postcodes in the lists. Thirty-two of those thirty-five also appear in Royal Mail's published list of non-geographic codes (poweredbypaf.com, "Current non-geos", July 2019); BN50, BN51 and BN52 (Brighton large users) do not appear in that 2019 list but meet the directory test (68, 9 and 61 live postcodes, all large user, one grid point each), so they are treated as non-geographic on the directory evidence alone. Two more are included on weaker evidence and flagged: CR9 (Croydon, 126 live postcodes, all large user, in the Royal Mail list, but spread over 60 grid points) and NG90 (Boots, Nottingham, 19 live postcodes, all large user, in the Royal Mail list, but on 14 grid points). They are kept out of targeting because nothing is lost by excluding them, but they do not pass the seven-point test. S1 (Sheffield centre) and NN4 (Northampton) also appear in the Royal Mail list, for a handful of large-user sectors, but are ordinary geographic districts (438 and 918 small-user postcodes respectively) and stay in the lists. The other candidates the automatic test raised (CB21, CB22, ME3, PE5, PE9, SN25, TN1 to TN5, TN20, WF15, WF16) are geographic districts where only one or a few large-user postcodes fall in the area; the whole-district pull shows hundreds of small-user postcodes at distinct locations, so they are classed normally.

**How to read the lists.** *Core* = at least half of the district's live postcodes are inside the neighbouring area. *Partial* = 10 to 49 per cent, with the share shown. *Fringe* = under 10 per cent, listed so nothing is hidden, but not meaningful coverage and I would not target them. For Google Ads, these districts are the second tier behind the full-value core lists: units sell there at 0.75 of their value, so a developer in these districts can still use the bank, but a nearer bank with full-value coverage will usually be preferred.

**Source files.** The CSV beside this file holds every district with its counts. The client-facing Word document is `Biofarm-habitat-bank-service-area-postcodes.docx` (both tiers in one file, one tier per district); it is built from this CSV, the full-value CSV and the neighbouring-area lists in this sheet by `tools/postcode-docs/build_docx.py`, and `tools/postcode-docs/audit_check.py` re-pulls the source data and compares it with the finished document (see the README there). Live map polygons for the check come from each bank page's own map data (`<BANK>_SRM`), re-fetched by the audit pull.

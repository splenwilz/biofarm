Overview

A landscape in recovery

Sleight Farm is a 70-hectare registered habitat bank in South West England. Once a heavily grazed sheep and goat farm, the site is being carefully managed to allow species-rich grassland to re-establish — creating high-quality, locally available Biodiversity Net Gain for nearby development.

Through conservation grazing, carefully timed hay cuts and ongoing monitoring, the grasslands are beginning to reveal what remains within the site’s natural seed bank. Recent surveys recorded lowland meadow indicator species and the first bee orchid on the site.

About the land & its legacy

From grazing land to living meadow

For years, much of Sleight Farm supported relatively species-poor grassland under heavy grazing. Rather than force change, our management has focused on creating the conditions for nature to recover — letting the grasslands re-establish and reveal the diversity held within the soil.

That patience is beginning to pay off. Areas once classified as modified grassland are developing the characteristics of more diverse, neutral grassland, while one parcel is moving towards lowland meadow.

Ecological ambition & long-term stewardship

Managed for at least 30 years

Species-rich grassland

Establishing lowland meadow through conservation grazing, timed hay cuts and green-hay spreading of locally adapted wildflower seed.

Structural diversity

Grazing by ponies and cattle creates variation in sward height and structure, supporting invertebrates, small mammals and reptiles.

Evidenced monitoring

Ongoing botanical and wildlife monitoring tracks progress year on year — from meadow indicator species to barn owls and a juvenile tawny owl.

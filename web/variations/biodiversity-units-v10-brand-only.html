When is off site Biodiversity Net Gain the right choice?

Every development is different, and the most appropriate Biodiversity Net Gain strategy will depend on the type of development, the characteristics of the site and the applicable regulatory requirements.

Biofarm works with developers to identify the most appropriate route to achieving Biodiversity Net Gain. Depending on the project, this may include on-site habitat creation or enhancement, off-site biodiversity units, or a combination of both.

Where off-site delivery is the right solution, Biofarm provides high-quality biodiversity units through our professionally managed habitat banks, helping developers meet their obligations efficiently and with confidence.

What is the difference between biodiversity units and statutory biodiversity credits?

Biodiversity units are created through habitat banks such as those developed and managed by Biofarm.

Statutory biodiversity credits are provided by Government and are intended as a last resort where suitable market units are not available.

What is the difference between biodiversity units and carbon credits?

Although both support environmental outcomes, they serve different purposes.

Biodiversity units are used to measure and deliver Biodiversity Net Gain and help developments meet planning requirements.

Carbon credits measure the reduction or removal of greenhouse gas emissions and are used to help organisations manage their carbon footprint.

While the same habitat may provide both biodiversity and carbon benefits, they are different products, measured in different ways and created for different purposes.

Every biodiversity unit Biofarm provides comes from a real habitat that we create, restore and manage for at least 30 years.

What is the Spatial Risk Multiplier?

The Spatial Risk Multiplier recognises that biodiversity delivered closer to a development generally provides greater ecological value than biodiversity delivered further away.

Why does location matter?

Choosing a locally appropriate Biofarm Habitat Bank can help reduce planning risk, avoid unnecessary additional unit requirements and support better outcomes for nature.

How much do biodiversity units cost?

Costs depend on habitat type, location and the number of units required.

## What the August 2026 BNG changes mean for developers

The rules around mandatory Biodiversity Net Gain changed on 6 August 2026 —
and for most smaller schemes, they changed in your favour. Here's what's
different, what isn't, and what it means for your next application.

### What's changed

Four changes apply to planning applications made on or after 6 August 2026:

**1. Minor sites can go straight to off-site.** The biodiversity gain
hierarchy — the sequence that told developers to deliver gains on their own
site before looking elsewhere — has been relaxed for minor development. If
your site is above 0.2 hectares and classed as minor, off-site biodiversity
gains now carry the same weight as on-site, from the first instance. You no
longer need to demonstrate that you exhausted on-site options before buying
off-site units.

**2. The smallest sites are exempt.** Developments with a site area of 0.2
hectares or below are no longer subject to mandatory BNG, unless priority
habitat on the site would be negatively affected. DEFRA expects this to take
more than half of the smallest planning applications currently delivering
mandatory BNG out of the requirement.

**3. Temporary development is exempt.** Schemes where the land is reinstated
within five years or less are out of scope — again, unless priority habitat
is affected.

**4. The self-build exemption has gone.** Self-build and custom-build
developments are no longer automatically exempt. In practice, many will now
qualify under the new small-sites exemption instead — it depends on site
area, not build type.

### What "off-site first" actually means for your scheme

Until now, even small developers were expected to carve habitat out of their
own masterplans — a pond here, a wildflower strip there — before off-site
delivery was considered acceptable. That meant design compromise, ongoing
management obligations, and negotiation with the planning authority about
whether you'd tried hard enough on-site.

Under the new hierarchy, a minor site above 0.2 hectares can plan for what
the site is actually for, and meet its full BNG obligation through registered
off-site units from a habitat bank — secured legally and managed for at least
30 years by someone whose whole job is habitat.

That tends to mean:

- **Less design compromise** — your developable area stays developable.
- **More certainty at planning** — a unit purchase from an established
  habitat bank is a clean, evidenceable line in your biodiversity gain plan.
- **No long tail** — the 30-year management, monitoring and reporting
  obligations sit with the habitat bank, not with you or your buyers.

### What hasn't changed

The core of mandatory BNG stands. Non-exempt development in England still
needs to deliver at least a 10% biodiversity gain, secured for at least 30
years. Major developments still follow the existing hierarchy, with on-site
delivery preferred first. And the statutory framework — the metric, the
biodiversity gain plan, the register — all still applies.

One transitional point worth knowing: the new rules apply to planning
applications **made on or after 6 August 2026**. Applications submitted
before that date, and existing permissions, continue under the previous
rules.

### Where your scheme lands

- **0.2ha or below, no priority habitat?** Likely exempt — but check before
  you assume.
- **Minor site above 0.2ha?** You can now secure off-site units from day
  one. This is the change that matters commercially.
- **Major site?** Nothing has changed for you — but off-site delivery
  remains available where on-site can't meet the full requirement.

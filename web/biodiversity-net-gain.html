What is Biodiversity Net Gain?

Biodiversity Net Gain is an approach to development that leaves biodiversity in a measurably better state than it was before development began.

For most developments in England, this means achieving at least a 10 percent increase in biodiversity, measured using the statutory biodiversity metric.

The principle is simple.

Development should not only reduce its impact on nature. Done well, it creates places where people, wildlife and communities can all thrive.

Who does Biodiversity Net Gain apply to?

Biodiversity Net Gain is mandatory for most qualifying developments in England, although it does not apply to every project.

Whether BNG applies depends on factors including the type, size and location of the development, together with the habitats affected. Some developments are exempt under the current regulations, including certain smaller sites and temporary developments, provided the relevant conditions are met.

If you’re unsure whether your development is subject to Biodiversity Net Gain requirements, speak to Biofarm early. We’ll help you understand your obligations and identify the most appropriate solution for your project.

Why location matters

One of the biggest misconceptions about Biodiversity Net Gain is that biodiversity units can come from anywhere. Location matters.

The statutory biodiversity metric recognises this through the Spatial Risk Multiplier. Where biodiversity is delivered closer to a development, it generally provides greater ecological value than biodiversity delivered further away.

Choosing a locally appropriate Biofarm habitat bank can help reduce additional unit requirements, support local planning priorities and create better outcomes for nature.

That is why we are building a growing network of habitat banks across England.

Is Biodiversity Net Gain mandatory?

For most developments in England, Biodiversity Net Gain is now a mandatory part of the planning process. There are some exemptions, but most qualifying developments must demonstrate at least a 10 percent increase in biodiversity.

Are all developments required to deliver Biodiversity Net Gain?

No. While Biodiversity Net Gain is mandatory for most qualifying developments in England, some developments are exempt under the current regulations.

These include:

  • Certain developments on sites of 0.2 hectares or less, where priority habitat is not affected.
  • Certain temporary developments lasting five years or less, subject to the priority habitat provisions.
  • Other exemptions set out in the relevant legislation.

If you’re unsure whether your development qualifies for an exemption, speak to Biofarm early. We’ll help you understand your requirements and, where Biodiversity Net Gain applies, identify the right solution for your project.

Can Biodiversity Net Gain be delivered on site?

Yes. Many developments deliver some or all of their Biodiversity Net Gain on site.

Where additional biodiversity units are required, Biofarm provides off site Biodiversity Net Gain through our own habitat banks.

What are biodiversity units?

Biodiversity units measure the biodiversity value created through habitat enhancement. Read more about Biodiversity Units.

How is Biodiversity Net Gain measured?

Biodiversity Net Gain is measured using the statutory biodiversity metric. Learn more about the Biodiversity Metric.

What is the Spatial Risk Multiplier?

The Spatial Risk Multiplier recognises that biodiversity delivered closer to a development generally provides greater ecological value than biodiversity delivered further away. Learn more about the Spatial Risk Multiplier on the Biodiversity Metric page.

What is the difference between biodiversity units and carbon credits?

Although both support environmental outcomes, they serve different purposes.

Biodiversity units are used to measure and deliver Biodiversity Net Gain and help developments meet planning requirements.

Carbon credits measure the reduction or removal of greenhouse gas emissions and are used to help organisations manage their carbon footprint.

While the same habitat may provide both biodiversity and carbon benefits, they are different products, measured in different ways and created for different purposes.

What is a habitat bank?

Habitat banks are areas of land managed to create measurable biodiversity gains that generate biodiversity units.

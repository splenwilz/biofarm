What is Biodiversity Net Gain?

Biodiversity Net Gain is an approach to development that leaves biodiversity in a measurably better state than it was before development began.

For most developments in England, that means at least a 10 percent increase in biodiversity, measured using the statutory biodiversity metric.

Biodiversity Net Gain at a glance

  • Biodiversity Net Gain is mandatory for most qualifying developments in England, subject to defined exemptions.

  • Most qualifying developments must achieve at least a 10 percent increase in biodiversity, measured using the statutory biodiversity metric.

  • Biodiversity Net Gain can be delivered on site, off site or through a combination of both, depending on your development.

  • Off site habitats must be managed, monitored and reported on for at least 30 years.

Biodiversity Net Gain in practice

Every development is different. Some sites achieve Biodiversity Net Gain within the development itself; others need additional biodiversity created elsewhere through professionally managed habitat banks.

Biofarm does more than advise. We create it — working with landowners across England to identify suitable land, restore habitats and generate off site biodiversity units through our own habitat banks.

Why location matters

Habitat closer to your development counts for more. The statutory biodiversity metric recognises this through the Spatial Risk Multiplier — so we create habitat banks near the places that need them.
